1 · Product overview ~1 min · slides 1–3

Slide 1 · Title
NarratorGrid helps a second-year grad student at their first career fair turn five forgettable hellos into real connections, and get better at it every time. You walk in with a goal, you remember what was said, you follow up, and your skills compound.
Slide 2 · Real connections. Real skills.
NarratorFor a second-year grad student who meets five people at a career fair and is forgotten by all of them, Grid helps them walk in with a goal, remember what was said, follow up, and get better at it every time. That's the product. The features are in the demo.
Slide 3 · You do the talking. Grid keeps the thread.
NarratorFive beats. Two of them have AI in them, the prep brief and the debrief, and in both the AI proposes and you decide. Nothing is recorded during the chat. Nothing is sent for you. Everything you write feeds Grid Insights, Ask Grid, and your Skills. Nothing else does.
Trap to avoid: don't list features. The screenshots do that. One sentence per slide, then move.

2 · User need ~2 min · slides 4–5

Slide 4 · They want to be remembered.
NarratorWe ran two recorded interviews. Not enough yet, and we'll come back to that. But what we heard was consistent. The conversation itself is not the problem. Read two quotes off the slide, slowly. "I forget what they told me." "They're so messy because you're not focusing on the laptop." And the one that shaped the whole build: "I would never pull out an AI note taker during the conversation."
Slide 5 · Seven branches. One we heard twice.
NarratorOur opportunity tree has one rule: no quote, no branch. Branch C, "I leave with notes I can't use and details I lose," is the only one two people described unprompted. That is where Grid's debrief lives. Since Week 3 the need moved in two ways. Nobody mentioned getting better over time, so our original North Star, AI-awarded skill points, is gone. And both people dislike AI the other person can see, so Grid never records, never looks anyone up, never sends.

3 · Value proposition ~1 min · slide 6

Slide 6 · Better than what?
NarratorThe current alternative is LinkedIn as an address book, a Notes app you never reopen, and asking ChatGPT for "good questions" that sound like everyone else's. Grid ties prep to one person and one goal, sorts your notes against what you wanted to learn, and refuses to score the people you met. AI earns its place in two moments: turning a goal and a person into questions in seconds, and turning a brain-dump into takeaways with the sentence they came from. Without a model key it still works, in labelled mock mode.

Slide 8 · Control map
NarratorEverything you just saw sits in two columns. The human decides: the goal, the edits, the send. The AI proposes: the brief, the takeaways, the drafts, and scores only when it can quote your notes. The third column is empty on purpose. Since Week 4 we drifted three times: calendar read dropped, AI-awarded skill percentages replaced with evidence-only scores, and Discover added, mutual connections only.
Slide 9 · You see it. You fix it.
NarratorWhen the AI is wrong, it's a checkbox you own. When it's unsure, it says "Not enough info" instead of a number. Every AI block is labelled. The framing question we expect: has the build pushed a hard judgment onto the human at the worst moment? The hardest judgment left is "does this sound like me?", and it lands three days before the fair, not in the conversation. Tell us if you disagree.

5 · Success metrics ~1.5 min · slide 10

Slide 10 · North Star
NarratorOur North Star is completed conversation loops: a chat that was prepared, debriefed, and followed up within seven days. It maps straight onto the need, lost notes and lost follow-ups. Sign-ups and page views don't count. Here are the honest numbers. Two interviews; the protocol asks for three or four. Zero C1 results; the sorted-notes-versus-summary test is wired but not run. Zero accessibility violations. Fifty-one tests, three of them stale. Not yet instrumented: prep created, debrief completed, follow-up sent. If fewer than a third of prepared chats get debriefed, people want the prep and not the notebook, and we're a question generator.

6 · Risks and next steps ~1.5 min · slides 11–12

Slide 11 · Risk · ask · next
NarratorBiggest risk: people over-trust the prep and walk in scripted. Smooth is harder to see than wrong. The seams, before you find them: the AI in this build is mock mode, Discover is seeded, Supabase and Stripe are untested, the rate limiter is per-process. Our ask: is "completed loops" the right North Star, or does it reward logging over relationships? Weeks 8 to 11, in order: run C1 with three people and two more interviews; evals with a real model; risk and governance, including the person who is in the system but never in the room; instrument the North Star. First commitment after Reading Week: C1, three people, results in the repo.
Slide 12 · Build & iteration
NarratorHow we got here: Mentra had AI-awarded scores and the Week 4 review flagged all three red items as places the AI decided. Sorted notes fixed that. Editable questions came from the interviews. Grid is the version where the human owns every decision.
Slide 13 · Ready to get on Grid?
NarratorScan to open it. Log in with any email. Now tell us what you see.
